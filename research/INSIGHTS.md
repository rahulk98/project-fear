# FEAR project -- exhaustive insights (plain English)

> **Note (2026-10-18).** In this project's TruthfulQA-MC data the correct option is always listed first and options were never shuffled, so TruthfulQA-MC accuracy, calibration and probe numbers below measure how often the model picks option A. Only abstention rates on TruthfulQA-MC remain meaningful. See RESULTS.md section 5.

This file is the exhaustive plain-English insights document for the FEAR (Fear-Enhanced Asymmetric Rewards) research project. It was assembled from all result sources (results.md, training_details.md, reward_systems.md, log.md, FINAL_VERDICT.md, FINDINGS.md, DECISIONS.md, matched_population.json, cross_domain_probe.json, sc_selectivity.json, voi_precheck.json, utility_summary.json, and supporting research files). Every bullet follows a fixed three-part shape: a bold plain-English statement of the insight, followed by a plain-English explanation of what it MEANS for the project, followed by the cited source in parentheses. Near-identical bullets have been merged, keeping the strongest citation. All distinct insights are preserved.

## Core mechanism / Finding O

- **Under GRPO's z-score normalization, all 7 reward conditions (S0-S4, C1, C2) produce mathematically identical gradients in groups that have no abstention, because z-scoring cancels out any affine rescaling of the reward (z(a*r+b)=z(r)).** -- The whole positive-only vs symmetric reward debate is mathematically empty under GRPO normalization; reward asymmetry can only act through groups that mix correct, wrong, and abstain outcomes, where it works as a single one-dimensional abstention-bias dial. This is the central "Finding O" that reframed the project. (Source: log.md, Session 19 (2026-05-26) Learnings; 'z(a*r + b) = z(r) for any a>0,b ... +1/0 and +1/-1 produce IDENTICAL gradients')
- **The entire alpha/beta/abstain reward design collapses to effectively ONE degree of freedom: the normalized advantage assigned to abstention in mixed-outcome rollout groups.** -- The apparent richness of the 7-condition sweep is mathematically illusory under normalization; only one knob actually matters, so the sweep is a 1-D abstention dial. (Source: results.md, Finding O informal theorem)
- **A worked example (group of 4: 1 correct, 2 wrong, 1 abstain) shows the abstain advantage is the only column that changes across conditions, rising monotonically S0 to S4 (+0.261 to +0.866) and C1 to C2 (+0.408 to +0.606), with S2 (+0.665) and C2 (+0.606) sitting close together.** -- The near-matched abstain advantages of S2 and C2 give a mechanistic explanation for why they behave equivalently, tying the abstract theory to observed behavior. (Source: results.md, Finding O table)
- **S4's abstain advantage of +0.866 is the maximum because with alpha=0 and abstain=0 the correct and abstain samples share the same reward, so abstention inherits the top advantage and a single abstention triggers a positive-feedback loop.** -- The catastrophic S4 collapse is mechanistically explained by the degenerate alpha=0 case creating a self-reinforcing "abstain is above average" signal (89.9% gsm8k abstention plus a 24% unconditional-refusal pool). (Source: results.md, Finding O empirical consequences)
- **The abstain advantage is the SAME for every problem in a mixed group, so the gradient carries no per-problem confidence information; only a global threshold can shift, per-problem discrimination cannot improve.** -- It is mathematically impossible for this reward family to teach finer per-problem confidence; the only trainable thing is a uniform threshold, a fundamental limitation of the design (this explains the discrimination-index collapse, Finding C). (Source: results.md, Finding O)
- **S0 and P0 are affine-equivalent ({+1,-1} = 2*{+1,0}-1) hence gradient-identical in 2-outcome groups; the entire behavioral gap comes from 3-outcome groups where P0 ties wrong with abstain (zero abstain-advantage) while S0 makes abstain strictly better than wrong.** -- The symmetric reward's positive abstain-advantage creates the trap; removing it (P0) or scrambling it (fear) escapes to the same point, so punishment is one interchangeable route, not the active ingredient. This is the cleanest mechanistic isolation in the project. (Source: log.md, Session 'P0 control result' Mechanism)
- **In S0, abstain (0) is strictly better than wrong (-1), so at low base capability where most attempts are wrong, the policy collapses into the abstain basin; in P0 abstain (0) equals wrong (0), so there is zero abstention pressure and the rare correct answers pull it toward attempting more.** -- The abstention trap is explained mechanically: it is driven by abstain outscoring wrong, not by any property of fear. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 P0 control section, Mechanism bullets)
- **Positive-only (+1/0) and symmetric (+1/-1) reward schemes are gradient-identical under GRPO normalization, so the field's empirical convergence on +1/0 (DeepSeek-R1, Tulu 3, Open-R1, SimpleRL-Zoo, Qwen-Math) is mathematically defensible AND arbitrary in surface form.** -- A widely adopted RLVR reward-design choice is shown to be both justified and cosmetically irrelevant under normalization, a notable theoretical contribution. (Source: results.md, Finding O)
- **The cross-condition representation transfer confirms Finding O internally: a correctness probe trained on any one condition decodes correctness in every other condition almost as well as the native probe (off-diagonal AUROC 0.989 math / 0.992 gsm8k vs 0.998/1.000 in-sample).** -- All conditions share essentially ONE correctness direction and differ only in abstention threshold, the mechanistic confirmation of Finding O at the representation level, not just behavior. (Source: results.md, Experiment 2 'Cross-condition transfer' table)
  **[Corrected 2026-10-18]** This transfer matrix comes from an abstained-versus-attempted probe, not a correctness probe (scripts/probe_analysis.py, cross_condition_transfer); it does not show a shared correctness direction. See RESULTS.md section 5.
- **Finding O holds only under scalar rewards; it admits per-sample-shaped rewards (where within-group reward values are not affine transforms of each other) as an escape hatch that lets z-score-normalized advantages carry per-sample information.** -- This refines rather than overturns the theory: confidence- or composition-shaped rewards (CWAR/OCP/CMF/GRF/SC) are the deliberate escape routes built to break the invariance. (Source: results.md, CWAR / refinement of Finding O)
- **The z-score affine-invariance was turned into a provable identity and shipped as a regression guard test (P5 invariance test, tests/test_advantage_invariance.py).** -- The team confirmed mathematically that z-score normalization cannot preserve reward magnitude, and froze that as a permanent check. (Source: training_details.md, avenues table P5 row; results.md Finding O, tests/test_advantage_invariance.py)
- **The S0->S4 abstain-advantage values cited here come from the corrected ddof=1 (sample std) table, not the earlier ddof=0 version, and the abstain-advantage conclusion is explicitly noted as ddof-invariant.** -- The team verified the worked example matches the trainer's actual std convention (TRL 0.15.2 `rewards.std(dim=1)` unbiased=True plus grpo.py masked-polarity divides by count-1), so the table is code-faithful, not just illustrative. (Source: results.md, Finding O worked numerical verification paragraph)

## Accuracy vs abstention trade-off

- **Asymmetric reward works as an operating-point dial that smoothly increases how often the model stays silent, but it does NOT make the model's answers more accurate per attempt and does NOT achieve the math-optimal policy.** -- The original research hypothesis was overturned: you can control WHEN the model abstains, but that control is not the same as improving calibration or answer quality. (Source: results.md, Revised project story)
- **Across the 7-condition sweep, accuracy falls and abstention rises monotonically from S0 to S4 on all four main benchmarks (math acc 0.459 to 0.158, gsm8k acc 0.556 to 0.060; math abst 0.142 to 0.613, gsm8k abst 0.173 to 0.899).** -- The central FEAR claim holds in direction: asymmetric reward trades attempts for silence in a controllable, ordered way (caveat: single-seed). (Source: results.md, Insights #1 and Full eval table)
- **Accuracy on the attempts the model actually makes (acc_on_attempted) monotonically DROPS from S0 to S4 on both math and gsm8k (math 0.536 to 0.411, gsm8k 0.673 to 0.592), the opposite of what selective abstention should produce.** -- The cleanest direct evidence that asymmetric pressure shifts the operating point without learning calibrated selective answering; abstaining more does not buy more reliable surviving attempts. (Source: results.md, Selective-prediction table / Key observation)
- **In clean greedy paired-causal mode (T=0, K=1, only LoRA weights differ), the S0 to S4 acc_on_attempted drop is confirmed real: math 61.8% to 51.3% (-10.5pp), gsm8k 72.2% to 59.1% (-13.1pp).** -- The worry that the drop was a sampling re-roll artifact is fully refuted; fear genuinely lowers per-attempt accuracy, and S0 still Pareto-dominates every other condition on both accuracy and coverage. (Source: log.md, Session 21 greedy confirmation; results.md greedy paired causal findings 1-2)
- **The acc_on_attempted decline survives four ruled-out confounds: selection artifact (drop persists on the all-conditions-attempt subset 0.626 to 0.493), truncation (completion length DROPS S0 to S4, opposite of a cutoff signal), format/extraction drift, and aggregation choice.** -- The negative finding is robust and verified by a high-reasoning codex audit, defensible as a real result rather than an artifact. (Source: results.md, Codex audit Session 17/18)
- **On the subset of 1326 MATH problems (26.5%) that ALL 7 conditions majority-attempt, per-attempt accuracy still drops 13 points from S0 (0.626) to S4 (0.493), a 21% relative decline.** -- Asymmetric reward hurts answer accuracy even on the easy problems the model always commits to, so the harm is not just from selecting harder problems away. (Source: results.md, Finding M)
- **The asymmetric conditions undershoot their target attempt-quality threshold by ever-larger margins as pressure rises; e.g. S4 on math has a 0.589 shortfall (threshold 1.00 vs 0.411 actual) and a 0.408 shortfall on gsm8k.** -- Higher asymmetric pressure makes the model abstain more but does NOT raise the accuracy of the answers it gives to match the implied threshold; selectivity rises without commensurate attempt-quality. (Source: results.md, Finding B table)
- **Only S0 (and C1 on gsm8k) achieves the expected-value-optimal attempt policy; every other condition's empirical per-attempt accuracy is lower than the threshold its reward design implies.** -- At this 1.5B scale the model never reaches the EV-optimal behavior the reward math predicts, so reward tuning sets the silence rate but not optimal commitment. (Source: results.md, Finding B / EV-threshold recovery)
- **S4 (alpha=0) showed the predicted degenerate collapse with extreme abstention (89.9% gsm8k, ~71% AIME, 61% math), exactly matching the theory's >80% prediction.** -- Setting the correct-answer reward to zero forces an EV-optimal threshold of 100% (abstain on everything), validating the EV-threshold framework; it is a multi-dimensional collapse (answer entropy 0.24 gsm8k, discrimination ratio 1.20, capability gap 0.46). (Source: results.md, Insights #2; Revised project story point 5)
- **S4 produces unconditional refusal (all 16 samples abstain) on 24.0% of gsm8k problems and 3.3% of math problems, while all other conditions sit near 0.1% or 0%.** -- S4 enters a distinct "give up entirely" regime at the problem level that the other conditions never approach. (Source: results.md, Unconditional-refusal pool table)
- **The decomposition of the small S0 to S1 acc_on_attempted drop (-0.92pp) is: +1.67pp from favorable abstention asymmetry, -1.3pp from attempting samples S0 abstained on (only 19.3% correct), and -0.4pp from sampling noise.** -- The favorable abstention effect IS real and positive, but it is more than cancelled by the model pulling genuinely-harder samples into the attempt pool plus sampling noise. (Source: results.md, Per-problem decomposition)
- **A more honest framing: per-sample calibration IS improving with the asymmetric reward (abstain-to-attempt flip accuracy drops 40% to 12% as S0 abstains more), but the policy cannot translate it into an aggregate acc_on_attempted lift because the threshold shift also pulls harder samples into the attempt pool.** -- Per-problem calibration improvement coexists with aggregate per-attempt-accuracy degradation, which weakens the blunt "FEAR is uncalibrated at this scale" framing. (Source: results.md, Per-problem decomposition; log.md Session 18)
- **A new "capability delivery rate" metric collapses dramatically under fear: math S0 96.5% to S4 58.5% (throws away 41.5% of math capability) and gsm8k S0 84.3% to S4 13.3% (throws away 86.7%, ~8,250 suppressed answers).** -- The cleanest single answer to "does asymmetric reward improve quality?": no, it shrinks the answers the model will deliver faster than it sharpens the correctness of those it does deliver. (Source: results.md, Phase 2 abstention probe finding 6)
- **On math the forced-correct rate among abstained samples CLIMBS with fear (S0 11.6% to S4 18.2%), but on GSM8K it DROPS (S0 59.8% to S4 43.5%), where even S0 already over-suppresses (60% of its silences are recoverable, ~8,250 correct gsm8k answers S4 still refuses to say).** -- Stronger fear pushes the model to silently abandon answers it could get right on math, while GSM8K shows the reverse: the baseline is already far too silent and pressure extends silence into recoverable territory. (Source: results.md, Phase 2 abstention probe findings 1-2)
- **Pass@16 (capability) is approximately conserved (within +/-1pp of base) for S0-S3, C1, C2 on math/gsm8k, and AIME-2024 Pass@16 actually IMPROVES (base 0.10 to C2 0.20); only S4 collapses.** -- Asymmetric reward is an operating-point operator, not a capability operator: the conditions keep the answer support and just shift which slice they commit to. This is the cleanest defense against the "GRPO destroys diversity" objection. (Source: log.md, Session 19 'pass@16 vs base'; results.md Pass@16 vs base findings 1-3)
- **S4 is the genuine exploration-collapse case, cutting Pass@16 from 0.94 to 0.53 (-41pp) on gsm8k and 0.79 to 0.62 (-17pp) on math, and also on multiple-choice (mmlu_stem 0.842 to 0.751, truthful_qa_mc 0.536 to 0.377).** -- With no positive reward the policy receives only loss-and-abstain gradients so capability is actively suppressed; S4 serves as an in-domain falsifier proving the pipeline can detect collapse when it exists. (Source: results.md, Pass@16 vs base finding 2 + table)
- **Capability is intact while willingness drops: the Pass@16 minus Pass@1 gap WIDENS with asymmetric pressure on math (S0 0.337 to S4 0.457), meaning the model can solve more problems somewhere in 16 samples than it commits to on the first try.** -- Asymmetric reward suppresses commitment, not underlying capability; the model "knows but does not commit" more under pressure. (Source: results.md, Finding E)
- **On problems where all 7 conditions always abstain, S0 still reaches Pass@16 of 0.223 on MATH and 0.889 on GSM8K (n=18, wide CI), so the answers are reachable in 16 samples and the abstentions are not capability-bound.** -- Models abstain on problems they can sometimes solve; the "reward-driven not capability-driven" framing is qualitatively robust but NOT yet causal (high Pass@16 only rules out capability-binding; isolating reward causality needs a KL-coeff=0 ablation). (Source: results.md, Finding N)
- **S4's answer entropy on gsm8k drops to 0.24 (near-deterministic) when it does attempt, versus 0.82 at S0.** -- Combined with ~90% abstention, S4 has collapsed to "abstain or commit to one answer," a diagnostic signature of the degenerate alpha=0 case. (Source: results.md, Finding G)
- **The greedy single-shot frontier gives the exact attempt/acc_on_attempted points: MATH S0 .867/.618, S1 .857/.617, S2 .849/.612, S3 .836/.602, S4 .325/.513; GSM8K S0 .951/.722, S3 .717/.699, S4 .017/.591.** -- Attempt rate drops monotonically while acc_on_attempted stays roughly flat across S0-S3, which is the cleanest single-shot demonstration that punishment does NOT preferentially drop wrong answers; S4 is the only large acc_on_attempted break. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 1 item 1)
- **Capability-preservation gap (Pass@16 - Pass@1) numbers are tabulated per condition: math S0 0.337 / S3 0.375 / S4 0.457; gsm8k S0 0.394 / S3 0.552 / S4 0.467; mmlu S0 0.586 / S3 0.579 / S4 0.566.** -- The gap widens on math (S0->S4) but on gsm8k it peaks at S3 (0.552) and falls back at S4, and on MMLU it is essentially flat, so the "know-but-don't-commit" widening is benchmark-specific, not uniform. (Source: results.md, Finding E table)

## Calibration & confidence

- **The original ECE was inflated by an apples-to-oranges comparison (Pass@K probability vs correct-any), where any problem with non-zero pass probability trivially counts as correct; the corrected metric uses mode-vote confidence vs whether the majority answer is correct.** -- A reported miscalibration number can be partly a measurement artifact, so the metric definition must be fixed before drawing conclusions. (Source: results.md, Finding A; log.md Session 12 finding #1)
- **Even after correcting the ECE metric (roughly half the magnitude), miscalibration still INCREASES from S0 to S4 on math (0.126 to 0.281) and gsm8k (0.236 to 0.386).** -- The harmful calibration effect on in-distribution reasoning is real and not just an artifact, because it survives the cleaner metric and replicates on seed=123 (max |delta| 0.016 across 12 cells). (Source: results.md, Finding A table)
- **The corrected ECE is flat on MMLU (0.251 to 0.276) and actually DECREASES on TruthfulQA (0.217 to 0.190) from S0 to S4.** -- The asymmetric-reward calibration harm is task-dependent, not universal, and even improves calibration on TruthfulQA. (Source: results.md, Finding A table)
- **The model is systematically UNDER-confident, not over-confident: in every condition-benchmark reliability bin the gap (mean confidence minus bin accuracy) is strongly negative, and asymmetric pressure widens it.** -- The miscalibration is in the "safe" direction (refuses more than warranted) not the dangerous one (over-claiming), so asymmetric pressure does not make the model brashly over-claim. (Source: results.md, Finding H; log.md Session 13 finding #8)
- **The under-confidence claim is caveated: negative ECE gaps under the chosen 10-equal-width binning at K=16, T=0.7 do not prove there is no over-claiming risk under distribution shift, adaptive binning, finer bins, or alternative confidence definitions.** -- "Safe direction" is only proven within the specific binning scheme used; the robust claim awaits the bootstrap / adaptive-binning / Brier / NLL robustness pass. (Source: results.md, Finding H codex-refined caveat)
- **The corrected-ECE claim is explicitly hedged: full robustness checks (bootstrap CIs per bin, adaptive binning, Brier/NLL companions) are still TODO, and saying "artifact resolved" overclaims relative to what has actually been ruled out.** -- Removing one leakage source does not prove the metric is fully robust, so claims should be hedged accordingly. (Source: results.md, Finding A codex caveat)
- **Trained conditions become more decisive: verbosity drops 28-36% and mode-vote variance falls, with calibration error sitting in the safe (under-confident) direction within the chosen binning.** -- Fear training makes the model terser and more conservative, erring toward under-confidence. (Source: results.md, Revised project story)
- **The apparent ECE/Brier regression under the fear-v2 variants is at least partly a binning artifact, because S0's abstain-everything regime mechanically lowers its ECE.** -- The apparent calibration regression may be misleading; adaptive equal-mass binning is needed before claiming the variants are actually less calibrated. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 OCP/CMF/GRF observation 6; reward_systems.md Deferred section)
- **On the math-specialized base, S0 training IMPROVES calibration (ECE, Brier) on in-distribution math/gsm8k but DEGRADES it on aime24 and truthful_qa (dece +0.0302 aime24, +0.0220 truthful_qa).** -- Calibration gains from S0 training are distribution-specific (better in-distribution, worse out-of-distribution), so calibration is not uniformly improved. (Source: results.md, K=32 base vs S0 deltas table)
- **The old (uncorrected) ECE values are tabulated alongside the corrected ones: math old 0.337->0.457 vs new 0.126->0.281; gsm8k old 0.394->0.467 vs new 0.236->0.386; mmlu old 0.586->0.566 vs new 0.251->0.276; truthful old 0.449->0.326 vs new 0.217->0.190.** -- The correction roughly halves every magnitude, and the old metric already showed mmlu flat and truthful decreasing, so the task-dependence is not an artifact of the new metric. (Source: results.md, Finding A table)
- **Specific reliability-bin gap values: S0 math [0.5,0.6) bin gap -0.268 (conf 0.532 vs acc 0.800); S4 math [0.5,0.6) gap -0.423 (conf 0.526 vs acc 0.949).** -- The concrete magnitude of under-confidence (a 0.42 gap at S4) quantifies how far the model's stated confidence sits below its own accuracy. (Source: results.md, Finding H bin tables)

## Selective prediction & the compute-aware result

- **Selective-prediction quality (AUACC) stays essentially flat S0 through S3 (math ~0.975, gsm8k ~0.997) and only degrades at S4 (0.914 math, 0.864 gsm8k).** -- The model does not lose its ability to separate right from wrong attempts along most of the sweep, so the calibration signal stays functional except in the degenerate S4 regime. (Source: results.md, Insights #4 and Full eval table)
- **Oracle-adjusted selectivity (within-attempt ranking, AUACC / Pass@K) stays intact and is even HIGHEST for S4 on math (1.21), gsm8k (1.35), and truthful (1.19).** -- When S4 attempts it reliably picks the easiest problems, so internal ranking quality survives even as absolute difficulty discrimination collapses; the two notions of selectivity diverge. (Source: results.md, Finding I; Revised project story)
- **On standard, compute-matched selective-prediction metrics (risk-coverage, selective accuracy, AUROC), punishment-oriented (FEAR) training shows no genuine advantage over the no-punishment baseline on any axis.** -- The headline final verdict: asymmetric/punishment reward gives no real selective-prediction benefit once you compare fairly at equal compute, making the project's core result a rigorous negative consistent with Finding O. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 1 VERDICT)
- **A single forward-pass linear probe on hidden states is a compute-efficient selective-prediction signal worth about 3 self-consistency samples (crossover K* = 2.5 on MATH, 2.9 on GSM8K), while RL punishment training buys no selective-prediction advantage.** -- The salvageable contribution: a cheap probe plus an explicit compute-crossover law, paired with the clean FEAR negative. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 2 contribution sentence)
- **After recomputing on the same problems with 2000x bootstrap CIs (matched population), the MATH probe (1 pass) AUROC 0.785 [0.763,0.807] significantly beats free logit confidence 0.563 and 2-sample self-consistency 0.725, equal to about 3.13 self-consistency samples.** -- Removing the cross-population winner's curse, the probe still holds up: one cheap forward pass buys roughly 3 self-consistency samples of selective-prediction quality on MATH. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Addendum 2; matched_population.json math_test)
- **On GSM8K the matched-population probe AUROC is 0.720 [0.682,0.756] vs free logit 0.595 and SC K2 0.637, equal to about 3.68 self-consistency samples.** -- The probe's compute-equivalence is consistent across both reasoning benchmarks (about 3-3.7 samples), supporting the K*(c) law as a robust quantitative result. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Addendum 2; matched_population.json gsm8k_test)
- **Free logit confidence (raw output-token probability) is a very weak selective-prediction signal, near chance: AUROC 0.563 on MATH and 0.595 on GSM8K.** -- Raw output probability barely separates correct from wrong, which is exactly why a learned probe on hidden states adds value and why this is the relevant cheap baseline. (Source: matched_population.json)
- **Self-consistency AUROC scales with samples up to a ceiling: MATH K2 0.725 / K4 0.831 / K8 0.878 / K16 0.904; GSM8K K2 0.637 / K4 0.736 / K8 0.816 / K16 0.850.** -- This is the explicit curve against which the probe is amortized; self-consistency keeps improving with compute but with diminishing returns. (Source: matched_population.json sc block)
- **Self-consistency overtakes both the probe and FEAR at a modest crossover of about 2.5-2.9 samples, not a 16x compute blowout.** -- You only need about 3 sampled answers for self-consistency to beat the single-pass methods, so the probe's compute advantage is small and bounded. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 1 item 3)
- **At single-shot (K=1) the probe and FEAR tie on MATH selective prediction (anchored gaps inside the +/-0.015 CI, raw gaps negative), with the probe having only a modest edge on GSM8K.** -- Neither method has a real single-pass selective-prediction edge on MATH; any apparent gap is within noise. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 1 item 2)
- **On the matched-K=16 risk-coverage (AURC) frontier, the non-fear escapes P0 (0.107/0.041 math/gsm8k), CW (0.108/0.046), and D1low (0.109/0.046) have the BEST confidence ranking, and S4 is the worst by 2.3-5.7x (0.254/0.286); no fear condition beats its non-fear counterpart.** -- Confidence ranking quality is monotonic in inverse-fear, and C2~S2 (0.116/0.116 math) confirms the equivalence at a fifth orthogonal level of analysis. (Source: results.md, Matched-K=16 risk-coverage frontier (AURC))
- **High-abstention fear conditions DO win absolute cost-sensitive utility U(c)=acc-c*wrong at high cost ratios (S4 overtakes P0 at c>2.1 math, c>2.5 gsm8k), but this win is dominated.** -- Even where fear appears to win on raw utility, it is strictly beaten because thresholding the symmetric baseline yields lower risk at the same coverage at every cost ratio. (Source: results.md, Cost-sensitive utility U(c))
- **The cost-optimal selective-prediction policy is "train P0/S0 and threshold confidence," never "train with fear," because strong fear (S4) actively degrades the confidence ranking (S0 selective-acc@20 0.990 vs S4 0.918, @50 0.937 vs 0.749).** -- A prescriptive, Finding-O-anchored conclusion that survives the asymmetric-cost reframe a reviewer would compute first, undercutting the original motivation for fear-based rewards. (Source: results.md, Cost-sensitive utility conclusion; log.md offline synthesis 2026-06-04)
- **On compute-matched per-sample utility for MATH, post-hoc thresholding of P0 dominates: P0 K=2 per-sample utility at c=3 is 0.1235 while single-shot S4 is -0.3082, and even P0 K=32 per-sample stays positive (0.0125).** -- Once you fairly account for compute, cheaply thresholding the no-punishment model's confidence beats single-shot FEAR on every per-sample comparison; this is the quantitative backbone of the retraction. (Source: utility_summary.json compute_fairness math_test)
- **The probe method itself is not novel (SAPLMA, P(True)/Kadavath, semantic entropy); the only novel sliver is the K*(c) compute-crossover law amortizing one probe pass against K self-consistency samples on a common axis, plus the clean RL-abstention negative.** -- An honest novelty assessment that caps the publication ceiling: the value is in the framing and the negative result, not a new technique. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 2 Novelty)
- **The killer baseline a reviewer would demand is semantic entropy / P(True) / sampled-confidence at matched compute on the same population and axis as the probe, because the original probe-vs-SC comparison was cross-population (winner's curse).** -- The probe's apparent edge could be inflated by comparing on different problem subsets; the matched-population fix was needed to make the claim defensible. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 2 'Killer baseline')
- **Updated K* and SC AUROC values appear in two places with slightly different numbers: Part 1 reports SC AUROC MATH K2 .799/K4 .889/K8 .911 (probe .828) and GSM8K K2 .722/K4 .830/K8 .854 (probe .779) with K*=2.50 MATH / 2.88 GSM8K, while Addendum 2's matched-population recompute gives probe AUROC .785/.720 and K*=3.13/3.68.** -- The draft cites only the matched-population (Addendum 2) figures; the Part 1 figures are the pre-matched-population numbers on a different (larger, group-CV) population, and the difference between K*=2.5 and K*=3.13 is itself the winner's-curse correction in action. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md Part 1 item 3 vs Addendum 2)
- **The probe used for the cross-domain experiment was the S0 probe at best layer 22 with group-CV AUROC 0.828 on MATH.** -- The specific probe provenance (S0-condition, layer 22, 0.828 group-CV) anchors the cross-domain transfer numbers and is the apples-to-apples ceiling against which transfer is read. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Addendum header)

## C2-vs-S2 equivalence

- **C2 (symmetric plus explicit-abstain reward, threshold 0.75) and S2 (punishment-dominant asymmetry, threshold 0.80) at a matched ~80% EV threshold produce statistically indistinguishable per-attempt accuracy on all four main benchmarks (every bootstrap CI crosses 0, math delta +0.0018, gsm8k +0.0061), with abstention differences under 1pp.** -- The headline novel contribution: the EV-implied threshold drives behavior and the specific reward design used to reach it is interchangeable; this is the first answer to the implicit-vs-explicit-abstention question (caveat: single-seed on the S2 side). (Source: results.md, C2-vs-S2 head-to-head; Finding D)
- **C2 and S2 agree on the same hard problems (per-problem Jaccard ~0.70 on both MATH and GSM8K) and produce the same committed answer most of the time (mode agreement 0.84 math, 0.94 gsm8k).** -- The equivalence is not just statistical in aggregate; the two reward designs are behaviorally similar problem-by-problem, and S4 by contrast is a per-problem outlier (Jaccard 0.11-0.15 with everyone). (Source: results.md, Finding J; C2-vs-S2 head-to-head)
- **C2 and S2 produce nearly identical forced-correct rates on abstained samples (math +0.5pp, gsm8k +1.6pp), making the same fear-vs-uncertainty composition of silences.** -- A fourth orthogonal level of C2~S2 equivalence (after per-attempt accuracy, threshold recovery, and per-problem stacking), strengthening the ICLR-defensibility of the claim. (Source: results.md, Phase 2 abstention probe finding 3)
- **C2~S2 equivalence holds tightly under greedy decoding too: math diff 0.4pp and gsm8k diff 0.2pp on acc_on_attempted, even with stochastic noise removed.** -- The strongest evidence for the equivalence: explicit-reward and implicit-zero families at a matched threshold produce indistinguishable per-attempt behavior even in the clean causal test. (Source: results.md, Greedy paired causal confirmation finding 5)
- **The clean equivalence has a residual exception: on MMLU and TruthfulQA the abstention direction FLIPS (C2 abstains slightly MORE than S2, opposite the threshold prediction), and there is a small but significant MMLU accuracy gap of -0.008 (CI [-0.014,-0.002]).** -- A real (small) domain-mechanism interaction: C2's explicit +0.5 abstain reward becomes relatively more attractive on low-accuracy multiple-choice domains where attempt value is low, so reward design is not fully interchangeable beyond the threshold. (Source: results.md, C2-vs-S2 Bootstrap CIs; Revised project story)
- **C1 (threshold 0.60) slots exactly where its implicit threshold predicts in the gsm8k abstention ordering: S0 < C1 < S1 < C2 ~ S2 < S3 < S4.** -- Independent confirmation that the EV-implied threshold is the underlying mechanism governing abstention placement across reward designs. (Source: results.md, C1 placement / Insights #6)
- **The mechanistic explanation for C2~S2 is in the abstain-advantage math: S2's +0.665 and C2's +0.606 abstain advantages sit close together (Finding O worked example).** -- The behavioral equivalence is not a coincidence; it falls directly out of the two designs landing at nearly the same point on the single abstain-advantage dial. (Source: results.md, Finding O table)
- **The C2~S2 deltas on the corrected metrics are tabulated: math acc_mode +0.0052, abst -0.0053, ECE_NEW +0.0048, p(correct|attempt) -0.0011; gsm8k acc_mode -0.0030, abst -0.0078, ECE_NEW -0.0090, p(correct|attempt) +0.0015 (all <0.01).** -- The equivalence holds on the corrected ECE too (not just per-attempt accuracy), a metric the draft's bullets do not explicitly enumerate. (Source: results.md, Finding D table)
- **GSM8K C2~S2 Jaccard is 0.697 against a backdrop where S0-S4 Jaccard is only 0.022 (S0 2% vs S4 100% abstention), and S2-S3 = 0.517.** -- The 0.697 matched-threshold agreement is striking precisely because the surrounding abstention range is enormous (the most extreme possible spread), making the per-problem agreement non-trivial. (Source: results.md, Finding J GSM8K block)

## Internal representations (Experiment 2)

(Source: results.md, Experiment 2 cvi AUROC table)

(Source: results.md, Experiment 2 cvi AUROC abstention column)

(Source: results.md, Experiment 2 'Cross-condition transfer' table)

## Per-problem & difficulty structure

- **Across the 7 EV-ordered conditions, abstention "stacks" (a clean chain of attempts then abstentions) on 92.4% of MATH and 93.9% of GSM8K problems, 33-35 percentage points above the proper marginal-preserving null (~59%) at 25-99 sigma.** -- The sweep recovers a SHARED per-problem difficulty axis: each condition's abstention set is roughly a top-K cut of that axis, far stronger than independent abstention with the same marginals could produce. (Source: results.md, Finding K)
- **The naive uniform-random null (6.25% over 128 patterns) was rejected as too weak; the proper marginal-preserving nulls sit at ~59% because S4's high abstention rate makes many patterns trivially stack.** -- A high observed effect can look impressive against a naive null, but the honest comparison is a marginal-preserving null, which here is far more demanding (59% vs 6.25%). (Source: results.md, Finding K; scripts/stacking_null.py)
- **The recovered difficulty axis is human-meaningful: model abstain-count correlates with MATH gold level at Spearman +0.234 (p<1e-62), rises monotonically L1=0.64 to L5=1.93, and recovers a subject ordering (Prealgebra easiest 0.88 to Precalculus hardest 2.14) matching human intuition.** -- The model's abstention signal tracks genuine difficulty as humans understand it, not an arbitrary internal artifact. (Source: results.md, Finding L)
- **On GSM8K the abstain-count correlation with prompt LENGTH is only +0.047 (not significant), so the difficulty signal is genuine difficulty rather than a surface feature like prompt length.** -- Rules out a trivial confound, supporting that the model abstains based on real difficulty. (Source: results.md, Finding L)
- **All conditions keep the "abstain more on harder problems" signal (Spearman rho > 0 everywhere), but its strength shrinks as fear increases: a discrimination index (L5/L1 abstention ratio) collapses from 5.39x at S0 to 1.20x at S4 and the absolute hardest-minus-easiest gap drops from 0.19 to 0.11.** -- Asymmetric pressure makes abstention nearly uniform across difficulty (approaching decision mode-collapse), so the symmetric baseline is actually the MOST difficulty-discriminating one. (Source: results.md, Finding C; Revised project story)
- **Per-sample causal abstention asymmetries show favorable discrimination at low thresholds that collapses at high fear: S0 to S1 math wrong:correct abstain ratio 2.2, S0 to C1 2.5, C1 to C2 2.1, but S3 to S4 only 1.4.** -- At low fear the model preferentially silences wrong answers (good discrimination); pushing to S4 makes it abstain heavily on correct answers too, confirming discrimination collapse; the C-family shows slightly better discrimination than the S-family at matched-low thresholds. (Source: results.md, Greedy paired causal confirmation finding 6)
- **S0's per-sample abstention probability IS calibrated to per-problem difficulty: the abstain-to-attempt flip accuracy drops monotonically from 0.401 (problems S0 abstains on 1 of 16 samples) to 0.104 (9-16 of 16).** -- If abstention were random, every bucket would show ~50% flip accuracy; instead the gradient tracks difficulty, proving genuine per-problem calibration in the baseline. (Source: results.md, Per-problem decomposition table)
- **Multiple-choice benchmarks carry NO calibration signal: MMLU forced-correct is locked at 44.9-45.4% and TruthfulQA at 33.9-35.0% across all 7 conditions, with abstentions roughly equivalent to attempts.** -- On MC tasks the model picks pseudo-randomly when uncertain, so asymmetric reward has nothing to amplify, and the cross-domain claim is unaffected by reward strength on MC. (Source: results.md, Phase 2 abstention probe finding 4)
- **AIME is at the capability ceiling: all conditions show 0-1% forced-correct on abstained samples.** -- AIME silences are pure base-model limitation with no extractable calibration signal. (Source: results.md, Phase 2 abstention probe finding 5)
- **The stacking null has TWO variants with different sigmas: Null A (independent Bernoulli, observed marginals) gives 59.3% math / 59.0% gsm8k at 51 sigma / 25 sigma; Null B (column-shuffle preserving marginals, breaking per-problem alignment) gives 59.2% / 59.3% at 99 sigma / 43 sigma.** -- The draft mentions one ~59% null and "25-99 sigma" but does not distinguish that the 99/43 sigma comes specifically from the alignment-breaking shuffle null, which is the more demanding test of per-problem (not just marginal) structure. (Source: results.md, Finding K Null A/B)
- **Finding N gives the per-attempt accuracy on always-abstained problems when S0 does attempt: 0.088 math, 0.661 gsm8k (vs Pass@16 0.223 math / 0.889 gsm8k).** -- The very low math per-attempt rate (8.8%) on these problems shows they are genuinely hard per-attempt even though reachable in 16 samples, which sharpens the "reachable but not capability-bound" reading. (Source: results.md, Finding N)

## Token / compute efficiency

- **Asymmetric reward saves inference compute: S4 cuts math token usage by 36% (1519 to 976 chars) and gsm8k by 28% (1069 to 767) versus S0.** -- A real practical upside (less wasted reasoning on problems the model will abstain on anyway) AND a methodological control: truncation would predict longer completions, so the opposite direction confirms the acc-drop is not a parser/cutoff artifact. (Source: results.md, Finding F; log.md Session 12 finding #6)
- **Each 1.5B GRPO run costs about 9.5 hours of wall time on the single A100 (~22.6 s/step x 1500 steps), making throughput the binding constraint on how many conditions can be swept.** -- The serial single-A100 queue is why the full Finding-O escape program takes about 4 days. (Source: training_details.md, Finding-O escape program note)
- **Z3 (forced-commit) produces longer reasoning chains that add about 22% to eval wall time (11h vs ~9h S0 baseline).** -- Forcing the model to commit instead of abstain materially changes generation length, a practical eval-cost lesson and an indirect token-level signal that forced-commit changes behavior. (Source: training_details.md, qwen2_5_math_1_5b table row 5)
- **The V100 KV-cache concurrency advantage (388x vs ~50x on a 2080 Ti) translated to only a ~5-10% real eval speedup on K=32, not the projected 3-4x, because the workload is compute-bound not KV-bound.** -- Headline GPU spec numbers overpredict real eval throughput; batch=8 raising vLLM utilization buys nothing on this compute-bound workload. (Source: log.md, S4 K=32 DONE 2026-05-28; training comparisons)
- **The 23 offline "WOW" findings (A through O) were produced entirely without GPU by running scripts/analyze_rollouts.py on existing rollout JSONLs.** -- A large body of publication-quality analysis was extracted from already-collected rollouts at zero additional compute cost, a useful efficiency lesson for resource-constrained research. (Source: results.md, intro to WOW findings)

## Scaling (0.5B / 1.5B / 3B)

- **At the larger 3B scale, the D1 explicit-IDK channel and the symmetric-beta penalty buy essentially nothing in per-attempt accuracy over the positive-only reward (FD1, P0, S0 all within ~0.004 on every benchmark; math .418/.419/.422, mmlu_stem .183/.184/.185).** -- The 1.5B Finding-O negative result replicates at 3B: extra reward machinery is an operating-point dial, not a capability dial, at a second model scale. (Source: results.md, K=32 scale-up section; reward_systems.md qwen2_5_3b row)
- **At 3B, the only axis that moves between reward conditions is abstention: S0 (symmetric beta=1) abstains roughly twice as much as FD1/P0 on math and gsm8k (math abst .069 vs .025/.032; AIME24 .195 vs .078/.080).** -- The asymmetric sweep is again an operating-point dial at 3B, mirroring the 1.5B result. (Source: results.md, K=32 scale-up; training_details.md qwen2_5_3b rows 1-3)
- **On Qwen-0.5B all three structurally different fear designs (OCP, CMF, GRF) converge to nearly the same behavior (inter-variant max delta <0.005 on math, <0.025 on gsm8k) and roughly tripled math acc_pass1 over S0 (0.046 to ~0.173, 3.75x) and doubled gsm8k (0.084 to ~0.175).** -- The specific shape of a punishment-based reward does not matter at this scale; what matters is that it injects per-sample variance, so tuning the fear shape is a dead end. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 OCP/CMF/GRF observations 1-2)
- **The 0.5B fear "accuracy gain" comes almost entirely from a higher attempt rate (math abstention fell from 88% to ~24%), not from more accurate answers per attempt, which actually DROPPED (math 0.400 to ~0.227, gsm8k 0.437 to ~0.317).** -- The model answers far more questions rather than answering better, so the headline accuracy boost is a coverage effect that trades ~17pp of per-attempt precision. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 OCP/CMF/GRF observations 2,5)
- **On 0.5B the fear variants more than doubled Pass@16 on math (0.224 to 0.587) and raised AUACC +34.2pp on math, with top-80%-confidence selective accuracy +45.8pp.** -- When you let the model rank its own confidence, the variants give a much cleaner accuracy-vs-coverage tradeoff than S0, the strongest signal that genuine latent capability expression is higher. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 OCP/CMF/GRF observations 3-4)
- **The P0 control (positive-only, no punishment) lands at the IDENTICAL operating point as the three fear variants on every metric at 0.5B (math acc 0.1728 vs 0.171-0.174, abstention 0.236 vs 0.237-0.242, Pass@16 0.587 vs 0.584-0.590).** -- A no-fear baseline escapes S0's abstention basin exactly as well as fear does, so the improvement over S0 is NOT caused by punishment; fear is neither necessary nor special at 0.5B. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 P0 control section; log.md Session 'P0 control result')
- **The fear variants and P0 reach the same basin by three different routes: removing the wrong-penalty (P0), injecting confidence variance (CW/OCP/CMF), or injecting group-composition variance (GRF), all of which lower the abstain-advantage and arrive at one capability-bounded operating point.** -- Three distinct mechanisms reach one operating point, confirming the abstain-advantage axis is reachable many ways and is the single governing variable. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 P0 control section, Mechanism)
- **The P0 result is a double-edged sword: it DEFLATES any "FEAR improves model quality" headline (a no-fear baseline matches it) but STRENGTHENS the Finding-O mechanism contribution.** -- Reporting fear-v2 as a positive result without the P0 control would be an overclaim a reviewer would catch; the honest framing is that punishment is one of several interchangeable escape routes, not the active ingredient. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 P0 control section, 'Project implication (honest)')
- **At 1.5B, P0 = D1low = FD1 on calibration (math ~0.494-0.498, gsm8k ~0.694-0.696), and the IDK channel was inert (3 boxed-IDK in 160k math rollouts, 0 in 42k gsm8k), with FD1's abstention even slightly lower than D1low.** -- FEAR attempt-asymmetry plus a heavier silent penalty and an explicit IDK channel add nothing over the positive-only baseline; the D-family advantage over S0 is the abstain-advantage axis, not fear or the IDK design. (Source: training_details.md, D1+CW queue rows 3a-4; log.md 'P0 + FD1 evals done')
- **The SC self-consistency LOO-penalty 1.5B adapter abstains far more (14% math, ~37% AIME) while achieving higher per-attempt math accuracy (.462) than the 3B positive-only conditions (~.419).** -- The SC mechanism shifts the operating point toward more cautious, higher-accuracy-when-attempted behavior, but this is shown on a smaller model so it is not a clean head-to-head with the 3B numbers. (Source: results.md, K=32 scale-up table)
- **In the full 1.5B reward-family frontier, the non-fear escapes P0 and D1low/FD1 achieve the highest math sample-level accuracy (~0.494-0.498) and lowest abstention (~0.11), while S4 collapses to 0.158 acc / 0.613 abstention (and gsm8k 0.060 / 0.899).** -- Removing fear maximizes accuracy and minimizes abstention while strong fear causes near-total silence, confirming fear hurts rather than helps across both benchmarks. (Source: results.md, '1.5B reward-family frontier' sample-level math/gsm8k)
- **The full condition coverage spans four scales (0.5B = 8 conditions, 1.5B = largest set, 3B = 4 conditions, Llama-3.2-1B cross-arch pair), but depth (multi-seed, full condition set) is concentrated at 1.5B.** -- The sweep is broad across scales and one alternative architecture, but cross-scale claims rest on fewer conditions per scale. (Source: training_details.md, section headers)

## Cross-architecture

- **On Llama-3.2-1B, fear does essentially NOTHING: OCP is statistically indistinguishable from S0 on both benchmarks (math acc 0.0019 vs 0.0020, abstention ~0.93 for both, Pass@16 math 0.028 vs 0.029).** -- The dramatic basin-escape seen on Qwen-0.5B does not reproduce on a different base architecture, so the fear-v2 win is not a general result. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 cross-architecture section; training_details.md llama3_2_1b rows 1-2)
- **The cross-arch null is explained by a "rescue bound": fear only helps when there is reachable latent capability (high Pass@16) to unlock, and Llama-3.2-1B has almost none (Pass@16 math 0.028) whereas Qwen-0.5B has substantial reachable capability (Pass@16 math 0.224).** -- Fear-v2 is a capability-EXPRESSION mechanism, not a capability-CREATION one; its ceiling is the base model's Pass@K. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 cross-architecture 'Why - the rescue bound'; log.md Session 'Cross-architecture (Llama)')
- **The Llama null is corroboration but NOT airtight, because Llama's near-zero baseline is a degenerate testbed (OCP did not even change abstention, stayed at 0.930) with no headroom for any reward to show an effect.** -- The null cannot cleanly distinguish "fear is Qwen-specific" from "Llama is the wrong base for any math-RL effect"; a cleaner test needs a non-Qwen base with non-trivial math Pass@16. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 cross-architecture 'Honesty caveat')
- **The Llama null aligns with the documented Llama-family math-bootstrapping failure (SimpleRL-Zoo, Zhu et al.): Llama bases struggle to bootstrap math reasoning from zero RL while Qwen-2.5 was pretrained on a heavier math/code mix.** -- The cross-arch difference is consistent with prior literature on base-model pretraining mix, reinforcing that the result is about the base model rather than the reward. This is also why Llama was dropped as the primary model and Qwen-2.5 1.5B locked in. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 cross-architecture; log.md Session 7)

## Cross-domain transfer

- **The learned abstention threshold generalizes out-of-domain: TruthfulQA-MC and MMLU-STEM abstention both rise monotonically S0 to S4 (TruthfulQA 0.771 to 0.867; MMLU 0.483 to 0.602).** -- The model learned a general "stay silent when unsure" threshold rather than math-only heuristics, supplying the cross-domain calibration evidence needed for ICLR. (Source: results.md, Insights #3; Revised project story)
- **The abstention threshold learned on math-answer training transfers to a completely different task (judging the correctness of reasoning steps), but the competence at that new task does NOT transfer.** -- Confirms the reward family sets a transferable "how cautious to be" dial that is separate from skill, strengthening the claim that asymmetric reward acts on the abstain axis (Finding O); caveat is the judging task is zero-shot and absolute judge skill is modest. (Source: results.md, Judging temperature sweep section)

(Source: cross_domain_probe.json; FINAL_VERDICT.md Addendum)

## Reward-design escape hatches (CWAR / OCP / CMF / GRF / SC / VC / zhu_signed / global_mean)

- **CW (confidence-weighted) keeps S0's symmetric rewards but rescales each sample's reward by its own completion confidence (exponentiated mean log-probability of generated tokens), injecting per-sample variance that survives group normalization.** -- This is the key trick that lets reward differences survive the z-score collapse that Finding O describes. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table CW entry)
- **CWAR at 0.5B reverses the standard FEAR pattern on math: +0.66pp per-attempt accuracy at only +0.23pp abstention, whereas the 1.5B sweep always traded more abstention for LOWER per-attempt accuracy.** -- The first observed data point where increasing abstention does not cost per-attempt accuracy under GRPO with z-scoring, exactly the calibration trade-off Finding O predicted scalar rewards cannot produce. (Source: results.md, CWAR section / headline result)
- **The CWAR math result is NOT statistically significant: +0.66pp with paired SE ~0.7pp (z~0.94, p~0.35), gsm8k +0.11pp within noise, single seed; the +0.02 ICLR gate FAILED, the +0.005 directional gate PASSED on math but FAILED on gsm8k.** -- It is a direction, not a proof; CWAR is a plausibly working mechanism with the right sign on math but undemonstrated definitively at this scale. (Source: results.md, CWAR statistical caveat / falsifiability outcome)
- **The CWAR effect is small because the 0.5B base abstains on 88% of math and 81% of gsm8k even after 1500 steps, so the per-sample reward variance only engages on the 12-19% attempted completions; at 1.5B it would have 6-7x more signal surface.** -- The mechanism has very little signal surface at 0.5B, so the weak 0.5B signal does NOT refute the mechanism at scale. (Source: results.md, CWAR 'why the small effect')
- **CWAR adopted a no-vllm training path (~22s/step vs vllm's ~4.5s/step) after in-process vllm consistently OOMed against another user's shared ollama allocations, and uses non-silent source-rewrite patches that raise RuntimeError if anchors do not match (conf_cw observed 0.4-0.7, confirming a non-degenerate confidence distribution).** -- Hardware contention forced a slower but stable training path, and the implementation fails loud rather than skipping silently. (Source: results.md, CWAR implementation and hardware notes)
- **OCP (Overconfidence Penalty) adds an extra penalty to wrong answers only when the model was confident above tau_oc=0.5, scaled by lambda_oc=2.0, leaving correct and silent samples untouched.** -- OCP surgically targets the "confidently wrong" subset, the most dangerous error type, without changing uncertain-wrong errors. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table OCP entry)
- **CMF (Confidence-Modulated Fear) multiplies the wrong-answer penalty by (1 + gamma_cmf*conf) with gamma_cmf=1.0, touching only wrong samples, distinct from CW which scales both channels symmetrically.** -- CMF makes the punishment grow smoothly with how confident the model was, unlike OCP's hard threshold, a different shape of overconfidence penalty to compare. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table CMF entry)
- **GRF (Group-Relative Fear) scales the wrong-answer penalty by how many peers in the same rollout group got it right (peer_correct_rate), with delta_grf=1.0 and no extra forward pass.** -- GRF calibrates punishment by problem difficulty inferred from the group (getting a question wrong that peers got right is penalized more) and does it for free. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table GRF entry)
- **SC (Self-Consistency LOO) scales the wrong penalty by how many group peers boxed the SAME wrong answer (answer strings, not reward signs), distinguishing a learned misconception (consensus-wrong) from random sampling noise.** -- A distinction GRF's correct-rate signal is structurally blind to, penalizing consensus-wrong more than scattered-wrong. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table SC entry)
- **A precondition pre-check for SC found answer-agreement predicts correctness very well (AUROC ~0.94-0.96 at G=8) but the overconfident-wrong population is thin (only 5-12% of wrong answers are high-agreement; the model mostly scatters when wrong).** -- SC's premise (consensus signals correctness) is statistically strong, but the population it would correct is too small to move training much, a caveat on its expected impact. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table SC entry; results.md SC precondition pre-check)
- **The live SC eval ties S0 on per-attempt accuracy (.462 vs .535... SC .539 vs S0 .535) and only differs by having lower abstention (a different operating point), not better accuracy.** -- Penalizing confident-wrong answers via leave-one-out self-consistency does not improve which answers are right; it just shifts where on the abstention curve the model sits, reinforcing the 1-D dial finding. (Source: GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md; SC verdict)
- **The zhu_signed advantage mode uses the raw signed reward directly as the advantage with NO baseline subtraction and NO std division, making it the only group-aware-free mode where reward-magnitude asymmetry actually reaches the gradient.** -- The deliberate antidote to Finding O for magnitude-sensitive conditions (P2 and the vc family), giving up the variance-reduction benefits of group normalization; it includes a collapse guard that early-aborts when abstention exceeds 0.97 sustained over a rolling 10-step window but does not zero individual high-abstention batches (preserving S4's wrong-channel signal). (Source: reward_systems.md, Trainer-side patches zhu_signed row)
- **The S0_zhu_signed (unnormalized signed reward) 1.5B run trained stably to 1500/1500 steps with no abstain collapse, making the magnitude test viable, but its live eval was WORSE per-attempt than the no-punishment baseline (acc_on_attempted .519 vs P0 .560).** -- Removing GRPO's z-score normalization to preserve reward magnitude did not destabilize training as feared, but even this newest "smarter punishment" variant does not beat P0. (Source: training_details.md, Finding-O escape program row 1; GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md eval table)
- **Removing TRL's within-group z-score normalization (raw_rloo) did NOT recover Zhu's W-REINFORCE abstention behavior on the math base, confirmed on BOTH S2 (deltas +0.65/+1.61pp abst) and Z3 (+0.08/+0.00pp abst), both under the 2pp no-effect threshold.** -- A clean robust negative: GRPO z-score standardization was NOT the bottleneck suppressing Zhu-style behavior, so the divergence cause lies on other axes (reward shape, KL coefficient, base model). (Source: training_details.md, raw-advantage variant rows 2-3; log.md S2/Z3 raw_rloo DONE)
- **CWAR/CW beats FCW (confidence + FEAR composite) on every accuracy metric at 1.5B K=32 (math +2.5pp, gsm8k +11.2pp) with lower abstention.** -- Adding FEAR asymmetry on top of confidence-weighting hurts rather than helps, reinforcing that fear is not the active ingredient and per-sample confidence shaping is the useful lever. (Source: log.md, CW K=32 eval done 2026-05-31)
- **CW, OCP, CMF, GRF, and SC are all "escape hatches" that break Finding O's invariance by different routes (confidence variance on both channels or the wrong channel only; group-composition variance via peer correct-rate or answer-agreement), but SC's within-group variance vanishes for unanimous-wrong groups under z-score, so SC must run with raw_rloo.** -- The whole second-generation reward zoo exists specifically to make asymmetry survive group normalization; even an escape-hatch reward can be silently cancelled by the wrong normalization mode, so the normalization choice is itself load-bearing. (Source: reward_systems.md, Finding O paragraph)
- **The d1 family is a five-outcome system using a position-locked IDK marker inside the last \boxed{}, splitting IDK into "wise" (genuinely did not know) and "wasteful" (knew but said IDK) by re-running the model with a forced \boxed{ prefix and checking against gold.** -- This lets the system reward good abstention and punish needless abstention separately, using a counterfactual forced-decode that costs only ~80-120 ms per call (~1.5 calls per step at G=8, under the <10% overhead promise). (Source: reward_systems.md, Reward family d1 section)
- **The d1 reward hierarchy is locked as -silent_penalty < -beta < -mu_wasteful < 0 < +mu_wise < +alpha, with silent_penalty = 1.5*beta so staying silent is strictly worse than committing a wrong answer.** -- The ordering encodes a precise value judgment: not answering at all is the worst behavior, deliberately separating the cost of total non-response from the cost of cautious abstention. (Source: reward_systems.md, Reward family d1)
- **On Qwen-1.5B the d1 IDK channel was effectively unreachable: zero IDK emissions across both Option A and Option B prompt variants over 160+ training steps, and only 3 boxed-IDK in 160,000 math samples at eval.** -- The explicit-IDK design failed to activate at all on the primary model, which is why FD1xsil rerouted the wise/wasteful logic through the silent channel instead. (Source: reward_systems.md, Trainer-side patches FD1xsil row; results.md 'Finding: FD1 = D1low = P0')
- **FD1xsil extends the forced-decode to silent samples, splitting silence into silent_wise (forced-decode wrong; penalty 0.5) and silent_wasteful (forced-decode correct; penalty 2.0), seeded by an SFT warm-start to address the inert-IDK cold-start.** -- It teaches the model WHEN silence is excusable by applying the wise/wasteful counterfactual to the silent channel, with dedicated telemetry for the legitimate-uncertainty vs knew-but-did-not-commit split. (Source: reward_systems.md, d1 table FD1xsil row; Telemetry section)
- **FD1eq equalizes the silent penalty to wrong (both -2.0) to fix a global_mean failure mode where silent_penalty > beta made silent advantage worse than wrong, which drove the model to commit wrong (silent fraction fell 0.27 to 0.09 while wrong rose 0.33 to 0.88 by step 400 in run mb9ek0yk).** -- A documented training pathology: an apparently sensible reward ordering combined with global_mean centring can push the model toward wrong-commit, and matching silent and wrong penalties neutralizes the bias. (Source: reward_systems.md, d1 table FD1eq row)
- **The global_mean and global_mean_std advantage modes use a running EMA baseline (momentum 0.99) that breaks per-group affine invariance, another route around Finding O that lets reward magnitude matter across groups.** -- Replacing per-group centring with a global running baseline so cross-group reward differences are not normalized away. (Source: reward_systems.md, Trainer-side patches advantage_norm row)
- **The vc (Verifier-Calibration) family trains a Qwen-1.5B adapter to JUDGE whether solution steps are correct (PRM800K phase-2 YES/NO) rather than to solve math, reusing the asym correct/wrong/silent vocabulary so all existing reward machinery transfers unchanged, with a content-hash leak guard (D2).** -- Generalizes the abstention/asymmetry idea from answering to verification, a separate task where the same calibration question (when to decline to vote) can be tested with a deliberate train/test separation. (Source: reward_systems.md, Reward family vc section)
- **The vc family is an attribution triple VC (asymmetric + self-consistency) > VC_flat (flat penalty, no conf/SC) > VC0 (beta=0, no penalty), where VC-vs-VC_flat isolates the conf-modulation contribution and VC_flat-vs-VC0 isolates the mere-penalty contribution, and VC must run under zhu_signed (or global_mean) or Finding O cancels its magnitude signal.** -- The three-way ladder cleanly decomposes the total effect into "does any penalty help" plus "does confidence-modulation help on top," with the normalization choice a required part of the condition spec. (Source: reward_systems.md, Reward family vc; vc table VC row)
- **The VOI-GRPO offline pre-check showed no Finding-O escape: in unanimous groups (all-correct or all-wrong) the within-group reward std collapses to near zero (0.0009-0.0028) versus ~0.014-0.033 in mixed groups, so a value-of-information reward signal vanishes exactly where it would be needed.** -- A proposed smarter reward cannot help because the informative signal disappears in unanimous groups that GRPO z-scoring already zeroes; another dead-end escape ruled out without GPU. (Source: voi_precheck.json; DECISIONS.md table)
- **In K=16 S0 MATH groups only 11.9% are all-correct and 20.1% all-wrong (68% mixed), but the leave-one-out plurality flip rate is just 0.264 and the mean pivotal-rollout fraction only 0.054, and P0 groups are even less pivotal.** -- Most groups are mixed (where a VOI signal could act), but very few individual rollouts are actually pivotal to the plurality vote, so the exploitable per-sample information is tiny. (Source: voi_precheck.json K16_S0_math_test and K32_P0 blocks)
- **The CWAR confidence rescale requires an extra policy forward pass after the reference log-probs, whereas GRF and SC achieve their per-sample variance with NO extra forward pass; OCP and CMF reuse CW's existing confidence pass.** -- A real compute-vs-signal tradeoff among escape-hatch rewards: confidence-based ones cost an extra pass while group-composition-based ones are free, which matters for training budget. (Source: reward_systems.md, Trainer-side patches)
- **Two always-on trainer guards keep degenerate cases runnable: an empty-completion guard (safe argmax on a zero-width is_eos tensor) and a std-floor guard that zeros advantages when intra-group std < 1e-4, which prevents S4's alpha=0 from dividing by zero when all samples abstain.** -- The degenerate S4 case (and any all-identical group) would crash standard GRPO; these guards are the engineering that makes the stress-case conditions runnable. (Source: reward_systems.md, Trainer-side patches)
- **The d1 family has a wandb collapse alert that trips when the running fraction of idk-plus-silent samples stays above 0.5 for 50 steps, and every reward call repopulates a module-level telemetry dict read by the trainer's on_step_end callback (per-class fractions, mean rewards, IDK rate, forced-decode timing, variant scalars).** -- The system actively monitors for the always-IDK/always-silent basin during training and instruments mechanism-specific diagnostics every step, enabling the post-hoc attribution analyses the project depends on. (Source: reward_systems.md, Telemetry section)
- **The only remaining untried path to a possible fear win is to TRAIN a smarter-reward condition not derivable from cached data: probe-in-the-loop RL (shaping GRPO reward with the probe's ~0.8-AUROC per-sample correctness signal) or calibration-reward RL (proper-scoring-rule on a verbalized confidence token).** -- Every offline and cached-data escape is exhausted; only a new training run injecting a genuinely informative per-sample signal could still yield a win, and within-group agreement at G=8 is far weaker than the probe's AUROC ~0.8, explaining why GRPO cannot match a probe-shaped reward. (Source: DECISIONS.md Goal-1; GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md proposed experiment 1)

## The honest negative verdict

- **The verdict is that FEAR produces controllable abstention but NOT calibrated selective answering at the 1.5B + 1500-GRPO-step scale, with S4 looking like degenerate risk avoidance plus degraded answer quality.** -- A cleaner expression of the negative result that aligns with Zhu et al. 2506.01347 at small scale. (Source: results.md, Codex audit verdict #5)
- **The project framing was deliberately pivoted from "asymmetric RLVR improves calibration" (the original FEAR hypothesis) to "asymmetric RLVR shifts abstention thresholds without improving calibration: a mechanistic analysis of GRPO under asymmetric costs."** -- Rebranding the negative result as the headline (with the GRPO normalization reduction as the mechanism) turns the field's convergence on positive-only +1/0 rewards into something to explain rather than contradict, a stronger and more defensible research statement. (Source: log.md, Session 19 'Project framing pivot')
- **The "FEAR wins under asymmetric cost at K=1" result was found to be a loophole and retracted, because it used a hand-constructed cost metric and compared against no genuine single-pass confidence baseline.** -- A finding that initially looked like a real win for punishment was an artifact of how the test was framed, not a real advantage; it stays retracted. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 1; DECISIONS.md Goal-1 table)
- **The retracted utility analysis did find a benchmark-specific crossover cost c* above which max-punishment S4 beats S0 in absolute expected utility (TruthfulQA 0.92, MMLU-STEM 1.70, MATH 2.14, GSM8K 2.63), with disjoint bootstrap CIs at c=3 on MATH.** -- The loophole was not just a point estimate; it had apparent statistical significance under the constructed metric, which is why it needed explicit retraction rather than dismissal, with the crossover lowest on low-accuracy honesty domains. (Source: FINDINGS.md, Result 1 table; utility_summary.json)
- **The loophole survived only because a cheap obvious baseline (single-shot logprob/entropy confidence for S0 at K=1) was never run, since the cached rollouts store no logprobs; the later matched-population "free logit" baseline filled it and the win disappeared.** -- The FEAR advantage was honestly bounded to the single-pass regime and only looked convincing because the relevant cheap competitor was missing. (Source: FINDINGS.md, 'Open confirming experiment'; FINAL_VERDICT.md Addendum)
- **FEAR's one favorable hypothesis (its threshold transfers better out-of-distribution) was refuted: its coverage "stability" is just difficulty-blind abstention, and a post-hoc confidence threshold transfers essentially loss-free while every fear condition shows 0.078-0.104 drift in accuracy-on-attempted.** -- FEAR's apparent OOD robustness is an illusion of uniform abstaining, not real calibration; the cheaper post-hoc threshold transfers better. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 1 item 4)
- **FEAR's apparent win on cascade/routing was traced to a probe base-rate bug; after correction the probe ties FEAR at the natural-abstain point and beats it at every higher routing fraction.** -- Another seeming FEAR advantage was a baseline implementation bug; adversarial recomputation flipped a positive result to negative. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 1 item 5)
- **The positive-only no-punishment baseline P0 attains the best MATH per-attempt accuracy (.494), best acc_on_attempted (.560), and best AURC (.987) at K=32, exceeding the symmetric S0 and all tested punishment variants.** -- Removing punishment entirely is the strongest condition on accuracy-of-answers-given, directly contradicting the project hypothesis that punishment improves selective accuracy. (Source: GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md)
- **The consolidated fear-v2 conclusion is that none of its findings support a "FEAR improves quality" headline (per-attempt accuracy dropped ~17pp under all escape variants), and advancing the FEAR direction requires a reward that escapes the abstention basin AND preserves per-attempt precision, the one dimension every escape variant so far degraded.** -- The net defensible position is a Finding-O mechanism result; doing better is net-new design work, not a tweak of OCP/CMF/GRF. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 round consolidated conclusion)
- **The publishability decision is a conditional GO toward a workshop / ACL Findings / project-core paper reframed as "compute-aware selective prediction (probe-gate + K*(c) law) with FEAR as the rigorous negative," explicitly NOT ICLR/NeurIPS main track.** -- An honest assessment that sets realistic venue expectations and pivots the project from a claimed positive to a defensible negative-plus-cheap-probe story. (Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 2 GO/NO-GO)
- **The project proposes three coexisting publishable framings: FEAR as a deployment-time operating-point dial (simplest), the asymmetric-reward effect being domain-conditional on the baseline's pre-existing fear composition (most novel), and C2~S2 mechanism equivalence at multiple orthogonal levels (most defensible).** -- The negative aggregate calibration result is reframed into a multi-angle mechanistic story, with the C2~S2 equivalence unaffected by the calibration negative. (Source: results.md, 'Three publishable framings')
- **The whole positive-only-vs-symmetric question is salvaged into a mechanism contribution: the symmetric reward's positive abstain-advantage creates a trap, and removing it (P0) or scrambling it (fear) escapes to the same capability-bounded operating point.** -- Writing it up as "FEAR wins" would be indefensible given the P0 control; the defensible result is the mechanism, not a performance claim. (Source: results.md, Fear-v2 round consolidated conclusion 'Net project position')

## Reward registry (condition design reference)

- **Abstention in the main reward family is implicit: a sample counts as "abstain" only when no boxed answer can be extracted; there is no IDK token, and an explicit \boxed{IDK} is graded as a wrong answer.** -- The model learns to stay silent rather than to say IDK, so any abstention behavior is an emergent side effect of the reward shape. (Source: reward_systems.md, Reward family asym section)
- **The implicit confidence threshold the model must clear before answering is (beta + abstain)/(alpha + beta), simplifying to beta/(alpha+beta) when there is no abstain reward.** -- This single formula converts the reward triplet into a predicted "how sure must I be to answer" cutoff, letting conditions be placed at known threshold points. (Source: reward_systems.md, Reward family asym section)
- **The asym conditions span thresholds from 0.500 (S0 symmetric baseline) to 1.000 (S4 pure NSR), with S1=0.667, S2=0.800 (matched-threshold midpoint vs C2), S3=0.909 (Zhu lambda=0.1 analog), and S4=1.000 (degenerate-collapse stress case).** -- The S0-S4 ladder is a clean monotonic sweep of how cautious the model is pushed to be, designed so calibration trends can be read against a known threshold axis. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym condition table)
- **P0 (alpha=1, beta=0, abstain=0, threshold 0.000) is a positive-only RLVR baseline mimicking the field-standard +1/0 with zero punishment, forcing the model to commit on every prompt.** -- P0 is the control that answers "does any penalty at all help calibration," since with threshold zero the model has no incentive to ever abstain. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table P0 entry)
- **C1 (abstain=0.2, threshold 0.600) and C2 (abstain=0.5, threshold 0.750) form the explicit-abstain family that pays the model to abstain, with C2 matched to S2's ~80% threshold, while Z3 (abstain=-1.5) makes silence worse than a wrong answer to reproduce Zhu's forced-commit setup.** -- The C-family tests whether explicitly paying to abstain produces different calibration than the implicit zero-reward abstention of the S-family at the same threshold; Z3 inverts the usual incentive to test forced commitment. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym table C1/C2/Z3 entries)
- **FCW (FEAR + CW, beta=2, threshold 0.667) combines an asymmetric attempt channel with CW's confidence weighting, and a clean A/B against plain CW isolates whether attempt-channel asymmetry adds anything on top of confidence scaling; FD1 plays the same isolating role against D1low, and CWD1 composes CW scaling on the attempt channels only.** -- These composite conditions are designed to separate "penalizing wrong harder" from "weighting by confidence" and "explicit-IDK rewards," telling you which mechanism drives any effect. (Source: reward_systems.md, asym/d1 tables FCW/FD1/CWD1 entries)
- **D1low (mu 0.3/0.3) is a low-magnitude control with only a +0.05 calibrated margin over always-attempting (codex-flagged as thin), D1mid (0.5/0.5) is the balanced version expected to give the first signal, and D1asym (0.5/0.4) probes the always-IDK basin.** -- The three D1 variants are a magnitude/symmetry sweep designed to find whether explicit-IDK rewards produce calibrated abstention or collapse into degenerate always-IDK behavior; the on-paper attractor analysis preserves calibrated > always-attempt > always-IDK > silent, so any failure points to optimization dynamics not a broken reward. (Source: reward_systems.md, d1 condition table; Always-IDK attractor analysis)
- **Under FEAR (beta=2) in FD1, always-attempt expected value goes negative (-0.20) while always-IDK stays at 0.0, raising the implicit attempt-over-IDK threshold from ~0.65 (D1low) to ~0.77 (FD1), with a pre-registered failure detector (FD1<D1low and frac_idk staying above 50% = always-IDK collapse).** -- The strong wrong-penalty mechanically makes blind answering a losing strategy (the intended lever) but risks pushing the model into an always-IDK basin, which the experiment is built to detect. (Source: reward_systems.md, FD1 attractor analysis)
- **Five models are in scope with distinct roles: Qwen2.5-1.5B (primary, full sweep + multi-seed + escape hatches), Qwen2.5-0.5B (fear-v2 exploration platform), Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (Zhu reproduction), Qwen2.5-3B (scale-up), Llama-3.2-1B (cross-architecture probe).** -- The model lineup is deliberately structured so each scientific question is tested on the appropriate base, with the 0.5B as a cheap exploration platform. (Source: reward_systems.md, Models in scope table)

## Methodology & infrastructure lessons

- **The codex review gate (extra-high reasoning before any server-side training/eval) repeatedly caught real bugs pre-GPU: a device-0-vs-rollout-device dtype bug, dtype cache-key collision, VC step-0 crash, D2 leak-guard no-op under -O, AUROC NaN inflation, and more.** -- A cheap review is worth it relative to a 9-15h GRPO run that crashes on a preventable bug; the gate became a HARD project rule with a same-CLI-surface skip carve-out. (Source: log.md, Session 8 + Finding-O escape program)
- **Cross-condition pairs at temperature 0.7 with the same seed are NOT a paired causal experiment, because once two conditions have different LoRA weights the multinomial draw at step 0 lands on different tokens; only greedy T=0 K=1 mode recovers the per-sample paired property.** -- A methodological self-correction: the K=16 transition matrix is valid only as an aggregate problem-level comparison, so a --greedy flag was added to make per-sample causal claims, which then confirmed the acc_on_attempted drop is real. (Source: log.md, Session 17 'Methodological clarification' + Session 21)
- **Codex's findings audit at HIGH reasoning judged 5 of 9 headline findings OVERSTATED (none WRONG): ECE-artifact-resolved, C2~S2 interchangeable, multi-seed 30-200x, under-confidence safe-direction, and always-abstained-Pass@16.** -- The underlying observations were intact and seed-validated, but interpretation needed hedging with CIs and caveats, a discipline lesson that surprising numbers should be steelmanned and bootstrap-CI'd before claiming them. (Source: log.md, Session 15 'Codex peer-review at HIGH reasoning')
- **Multi-seed validation (seed 42 vs 123 on S0/S3/C2) showed condition effects are far larger than seed effects (every cross-seed delta <0.03, mean |delta_acc| 0.003, identical discrimination index 5.39 in both seeds), but codex flagged the "30-200x" phrasing as needing per-finding denominators, and seed=456 is still needed for full n=3 error bars.** -- The findings are reproducible across seeds with effect sizes 5-50x larger than plausible seed noise, but replication is still required for ICLR-grade error bars, and the headline C2-vs-S2 equivalence has no error bars on the S2 side (S2 not in the multi-seed plan). (Source: log.md, Session 15 multi-seed table; results.md Concerns #1-2)
- **The eval output filename scheme keyed on --condition but not adapter/seed/K, so different-adapter, non-default-seed, or different-K runs silently hit a "rollouts file exists, re-score" fast path and emitted mislabeled metrics (S2-raw_rloo re-scored cached main-S2 rollouts; a CW launch missing --condition re-scored base rollouts).** -- A latent provenance bug that produced fake "results" twice; fixed with an --output-tag flag, auto seed-tag, and a provenance sidecar that refuses to silently re-score clashing caches. (Source: log.md, Session 'Wakeup 36e0754c'; training_details.md D1+CW row 1, raw-advantage row 2)
- **Launcher scripts repeatedly mis-reported EXIT=0 even on Python failure (tee | bash and missing pipefail swallow the real exit code), and a watcher matched a smoke-completion line and prematurely killed the queue, skipping CW prod entirely.** -- All launchers MUST use set -o pipefail and EXIT=${PIPESTATUS[0]}, and watchers must inspect the output path (outputs/smoke vs outputs/grpo) not just the condition name, or autonomous pipelines silently corrupt the queue plan. (Source: log.md, Session 7 mistakes + Session 'Watcher misfire'; training_details.md D1+CW Incident 2026-05-29)
- **An extraction bug treated unclosed boxes like \boxed{42 as attempted answers, but it affected only 60-167 MATH samples and 0-4 GSM8K per condition (out of 80K/21K) and shifted values by about +0.001 math / <=0.0002 gsm8k.** -- The malformed-box bug was real but immaterial; the fix requires the closing brace, and ruling it out is a good example of finding a real bug that did not explain the fear-not-calibration finding. (Source: results.md, Re-score audit Session 18; log.md Session 18)
- **Orion's A100 + vLLM-in-process GRPO requires PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True, enable_prefix_caching=False, swap_space=0, and vllm_gpu_memory_utilization=0.30, or training hits a CUDA illegal-memory-access within ~12 steps.** -- These exact allocator settings are the decisive fix for the vLLM-KV-cache vs PEFT-training arena collision at the weight-sync step; without them long GRPO runs crash on a preventable bug. (Source: log.md, project context + Session 25; training_details.md Math Z3 row)
- **V100 (Volta cc 7.0) is unusable for vLLM-in-process GRPO because Triton 3.1.0 dropped Volta MMA layout support (Allocation.cpp:47 assertion), while Turing 2080 Ti (cc 7.5) works.** -- Hardware compute capability is a hard, non-fixable constraint on the training stack; the whole 0.5B-on-2080-Ti development path emerged from discovering V100 was the blocker, not the model size. (Source: log.md, Session 5 'Fundamental blocker discovered')
- **vLLM 0.6.4 plus accelerate single-process cannot do clean split-GPU rollouts on heterogeneous hardware (vllm hardcodes local_rank=0 while accelerate defaults to cuda:0, and --gpu_ids hides the other cards from vllm).** -- A hard stack constraint forcing either vllm sharing one GPU with the policy (memory-starved) or single-GPU runs; unsolvable in one process at this version. (Source: log.md, Session 5 'vLLM-on validation saga')
- **vLLM's borderline-VRAM failure mode is diagnostically deceptive: it allocates weights, shrinks KV cache, then crashes much later with "max seq len (2048) > KV cache (704)", which looks like a model-config bug but means another tenant ate the headroom, and the eval script hardcodes gpu_memory_utilization=0.85 so a card with even ~2 GB used by another user OOMs.** -- On shared GPUs with ollama tenants this must be diagnosed via nvidia-smi VRAM check, not config edits. (Source: log.md, Session 21 'Things I learned')
- **TRL's GRPOTrainer requires per_device_train_batch_size * world_size divisible by num_generations (batch>=G), and Qwen2.5's 152K-vocab fp32-logits explosion on a 32GB V100 forced G down from 8 to 4.** -- An API divisibility rule plus a vocab-size memory ceiling drove a documented methodology footnote; writing configs from memory misses such rules, which is why smoke and codex catch them. (Source: log.md, Session 3 codex bugs + Session 5)
- **Literal LaTeX braces in Python format strings repeatedly broke the code: \boxed{} in a .format() template raised IndexError, and P6's \boxed{{IDK}} produced \boxed{IDK} that was silently penalized.** -- Literal braces in any LaTeX template fed through .format() must be escaped, and these break only at the first .format() call, so smoke must exercise prompt-building before any training run. (Source: log.md, Session 3 mistakes + Finding-O P0 fixes)
- **log_completions=False during the original 7-condition sweep was a self-inflicted blind spot: no per-step rollout JSONLs were written, so per-GROUP reward std (which the std-floor guard actually operates on) could never be measured, only batch-averaged reward_std.** -- The smoking-gun Tier-2/3 mechanism verification (fraction of all-abstain groups over training) is impossible to do post-hoc; setting log_completions=True is a trivial config change future runs should always use, and the worked-table std uses sample std (ddof=1) to match the trainer (TRL 0.15.2 + grpo.py). (Source: log.md, Session 19 Learnings; results.md Finding O caveat b)
- **The cross-condition reward_mean differences in WandB logs (S2 ~-0.78 vs S0 ~0) are a z-scoring measurement artifact, NOT behavioral divergence, and the only macroscopic Tier-1 training-time signal is S4's completion length drop (-16%, 566 to 476 tokens) while all other conditions stay within a noise band.** -- Cross-condition reward_mean must not be read as behavioral differences, and the highest abstain-advantage (S4) is the only condition that drifts visibly during training, matching the theory's prediction. (Source: results.md, Finding O caveat a + Tier-1 dynamics)
- **The std-floor verification (fraction of "alive" gradient steps) was only weakly supportive of the basin-escape hypothesis: fear variants kept ~1.7x more alive steps than S0 (33-39% vs 20%), but all four spent the MAJORITY of steps near the floor.** -- The "S0 stuck, variants escape" framing is too strong (it is a difference of degree), and two confounds (batch vs per-group std, S0's sparse 20 logged points) prevent calling it proof. (Source: log.md, Session 'Std-floor verification')
- **vLLM determinism breaks only at the 3rd-4th decimal across GPU cards (2080Ti vs V100 deltas: acc -0.00028, abst -0.00105, ece -0.00004), confirming cross-card numbers are not regressions.** -- Evals are reproducible across GPU types to ~3 decimals, so small cross-card differences are numerical noise, validating that mixing eval cards does not bias reported numbers. (Source: results.md, footnote [^s0mmlu]; training_details.md Math S0 row)
- **The K=32 evals were deliberately rerun at matched K to supersede an earlier K=16 base-only run so the trained-vs-base comparison is apples-to-apples; the AURC frontier was likewise recomputed at matched K=16 by capping K=32 runs to the first 16 samples.** -- Cross-condition comparisons must control for the number of samples K, otherwise K differences masquerade as confidence-ranking or accuracy differences. (Source: training_details.md, K=32 eval section; results.md '1.5B reward-family frontier' header)
- **The self-consistency selectivity numbers were computed on different-sized populations per K (MATH n=4736 at K2 rising to 4996 at K16, mode_correct_rate climbing 0.505 to 0.635), confirming the cross-population winner's-curse that the matched-population recompute later fixed.** -- Comparing the probe directly against these SC numbers would be unfair because populations and base accuracies differ across K, which is why the matched-population recompute was necessary. (Source: sc_selectivity.json; FINAL_VERDICT.md Part 2)
- **The S0-0.5B wandb run id was mis-recorded as 4yn9a8kr (404s on cloud, an un-synced local attempt) and corrected to gsgyll78; the base-model eval row was completed on disk but never flipped in the tracker and had to be reconciled retroactively from on-disk metrics.** -- A tracker-integrity lesson: run-id provenance and eval-completion state must be checked against the cloud API and on-disk metrics, since local-only attempts and un-flipped rows silently break downstream analysis. (Source: log.md, 'Std-floor verification' / 'P0 control' Tracker correction; training_details.md qwen2_5_1_5b row 14, qwen2_5_0_5b row 1)
- **A latent eval bug at eval_calibration.py:118 caused a CW eval to silently default --condition to base and re-score base rollouts, and an eval filename collision between main-S2 and raw_rloo-S2 caches aborted a run in 54 seconds.** -- Silent eval misconfiguration (a defaulted condition flag plus filename-stem collision) can make an eval appear to run while re-scoring the wrong model; resolved by the --output-tag flag adopted across later variant evals. (Source: training_details.md, D1+CW row 1, raw-advantage row 2)
- **The SC condition was a resume from checkpoint-300 to 1500 steps, and resumed runs do NOT write intermediate checkpoints due to an HF Trainer save-schedule quirk; the 3B S0 eval had to be rerun after the original Orion run crashed disk-full.** -- Resumed runs lose intermediate checkpointing (spot-preemption fallback is the last pre-resume checkpoint), and disk exhaustion at adapter-merge time is a real failure mode for the K=32 pipeline requiring failover to the eval host. (Source: training_details.md, qwen2_5_3b row 4 + Notes; results.md K=32 scale-up)

(Source: results.md, 'SC precondition pre-check'; reward_systems.md SC entry)

(Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 2 "THE ONE DECISIVE EXPERIMENT")

## New results since the initial extraction (2026-06-05, loop ticks)

- **Once you stop normalizing the reward, the asymmetry finally changes behavior, but it over-abstains.** Under the zhu_signed advantage mode (raw signed reward, no baseline, no std, so the +alpha/-beta magnitude survives to the gradient), S2 abstains about 3x as often as S0 (math 46% vs 15%, gsm8k 80% vs 19%), the first non-flat signal past Finding O. But it bites toward degradation, not clean calibration: S2_zhu accuracy-on-attempted is ~0.16 vs S0_zhu ~0.52, and on out-of-distribution benchmarks it approaches all-abstain (gsm8k 80%, mmlu 93%, tqa 99%). Meaning: the reward magnitude genuinely was being cancelled by z-score normalization, and letting it through makes high-beta fear destabilize into over-abstention. (Source: results.md, P2 zhu_signed section)
- **Finding O, reframed: GRPO's z-score normalization does not merely HIDE the asymmetry, it STABILIZES it.** Raw magnitude reaches behavior (P2) but for high beta it produces over-abstention and instability; normalization cancels the magnitude and thereby protects against that instability, which is why the whole z_score sweep collapses to a well-behaved 1-D abstention dial. The normalization that makes asymmetry invisible is also what keeps training stable. (Source: results.md, P2 reframing of Finding O)
- **Training a step-judge with a confident-wrong / self-consistency penalty (the VC family) collapses to always saying YES.** VC = VC0 = 0.539 and VC_flat 0.554 AUROC (CI-overlapping), all with NO=0 (the judge never flags an error), worse than the ~0.57 zero-shot baseline. Meaning: under the zhu_signed mode, the confident-wrong-penalty judge mode-collapses to never predicting "wrong," falsifying the VC > VC_flat > VC0 hypothesis. (Source: results.md, Exp-3 VC reward family)
- **Fear is the abstention dial in EVERY domain, including adversarial and hard ones, not just standard math.** Tested whether fear's extra abstention is positively selected on misleading (TruthfulQA) or hard (AIME, MMLU) benchmarks; it is not. Accuracy-on-attempted from S0 to S4 falls on every benchmark (math -0.105, gsm8k -0.131, mmlu -0.026, truthful_qa -0.035, aime -0.05). Meaning: fear never improves the underlying selectivity in any domain; it only moves the operating point. (Source: research/fear_domain_split.py, research/outputs/fear_domain_split.json)
- **On TruthfulQA, fear lowers the rate of confidently asserting a falsehood, but only by abstaining, at a roughly one-for-one cost in correct answers.** S0 to S4 raises abstention (0.71 to 0.83) and lowers confident-wrong (0.165 to 0.104), but loses about as many correct answers as wrong ones it avoids (loses 0.057 correct to save 0.061 wrong). Meaning: the apparent "safer on misinformation" effect is the abstention dial again, not better discrimination. (Source: research/fear_domain_split.py / GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md)
- **On the proper selective-prediction metrics, no punishment condition beats no-punishment, and the metrics are saturated.** Area-under-accuracy-coverage (auacc) and selective-accuracy-at-50/80 sit at ~0.987 / 1.000 for every strong condition at K=32, so the reward-design choice is invisible on the selective metric too, another face of Finding O. Where the metrics do separate, more fear is WORSE (auacc S1 to S4: 0.975, 0.973, 0.974, 0.914). The only conditions that weakly edge P0 are the explicit-IDK family (D1low/FD1), not fear, and within ~20 problems. Meaning: the metric that should reward calibrated abstention cannot separate the reward designs, and fear only degrades it. (Source: research/GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md, selective-metric sweep)
- **Probe-in-the-loop for the SOLVER reward is conceptually weak: during RLVR you already have the gold correctness, so a 0.8-AUROC probe is a noisier copy of the signal you already hold.** The single-pass correctness probe is genuinely useful at INFERENCE (selective prediction, routing), but as a training-time reward shaper it cannot beat the verifiable gold it would be approximating. Meaning: the probe's payoff is at deployment, not inside the RL loop. (Source: research/GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md, probe-in-the-loop elimination)
- **On the project's CENTRAL application (using the trained model to judge other LLMs' reasoning), fear does not make a better judge.** The full PRM800K judge sweep (13 zero-shot solver-as-judge conditions, matched-coverage AUROC at detecting wrong steps, n=10,015) ranks mild S0 (symmetric +1/-1) top at 0.576 but its CI fully overlaps no-punishment P0 (0.573), a tie not a win, while every higher-fear condition sits at the bottom (S2 0.562, S3 0.560, C1 0.560, C2 0.559). More fear = worse error detector. Meaning: the original Exp-3 hypothesis (fear-trained solvers become better error detectors for other models) is falsified; punishment does not help on the very application the project was built around. All judges are weak (~0.56-0.58 AUROC) regardless. (Source: results.md Exp-3 / outputs/phase5/p1_judge_sweep_full_s42.json)
- **The reward-normalization spectrum is now fully mapped, and fear never produces selective abstention anywhere on it.** Three advantage modes span how much of the +alpha/-beta magnitude survives to the gradient: z_score (cancels it -> flat, Finding O), global_mean (keeps a little -> mild effect), zhu_signed (keeps all -> strong effect). At the high-fear end of each, going S0 to S2: under global_mean, abstention rises (gsm8k 0.28 to 0.50) but accuracy-on-attempted DROPS (gsm8k 0.648 to 0.625, math 0.516 to 0.494) and calibration worsens (gsm8k ECE 0.494 to 0.633); under zhu_signed the same happens but far more severely (acc-on-attempted collapses to ~0.16). Meaning: across the entire normalization axis, more fear only ranges from "no effect" (z_score) to "over-abstention with accuracy loss" (global_mean, then zhu_signed); it never converts into selective abstention. This closes the last untested empirical avenue and completes the negative result along the normalization dimension. (Source: research/GOAL1_LIVE_TRACKING.md global_mean comparison; outputs/eval/qwen2_5_1_5b_S{0,2}_global_mean_float16_*)

## Open questions

- **The "reward-driven, not capability-driven" abstention framing is NOT yet causal: high Pass@16 only rules out capability-binding, so isolating reward causality requires a KL-coeff=0 ablation (an S-condition trained with KL_coeff=0), with a matching KL_coeff=0 fear-v2 ablation also planned.** -- Showing capability exists does not prove the reward caused the abstention; alternatives (policy mass uncertainty, decoding instability, cautious priors) remain until the causal ablation is run. (Source: results.md, Finding N; reward_systems.md Deferred section)
- **The definitive smoking-gun mechanism verification (Tier-2/3 per-group rollout composition over training) is deferred because it requires retraining with log_completions=True (~$17 for 2 conditions, ~$34 for 4).** -- The strongest causal confirmation of Finding O remains untested for cost reasons, so the mechanism claim rests on the analytical derivation plus Tier-1 plus eval-time evidence rather than direct per-group measurement. (Source: results.md, Finding O smoking-gun deferred)
- **All major behavioral findings are currently single-seed except the S0/S3/C2 subset, and the OCP/CMF/GRF inter-variant convergence is single-seed (42 only) and needs bootstrap CIs to upgrade from a point estimate to a robust claim.** -- The headline C2-vs-S2 equivalence has no error bars on the S2 side, and the striking "three fear mechanisms converge" result must be treated as preliminary until bootstrap-confirmed across seeds. (Source: results.md, Concerns #1-2; reward_systems.md Deferred section)
- **The corrected-ECE finding still lacks full robustness checks (bootstrap CIs per bin, adaptive equal-mass binning, Brier/NLL companions), and the under-confidence "safe direction" claim is only proven within the chosen 10-equal-width binning.** -- The calibration conclusions await a robustness pass before the binning-dependent claims can be stated without hedging. (Source: results.md, Finding A and Finding H caveats)
- **Pass@16 was not yet computed for CW, so its +0.66pp math gain is on per-sample selective accuracy (acc_on_attempted over K=16 independent rollouts), not on per-problem "any of 16 correct."** -- Pass@16 would test a different (capability-ceiling/diversity) claim; CWAR specifically targets calibrated commitment under uncertainty, so the missing Pass@16 is a deliberate scope boundary, and CWAR is a different point on the same risk-coverage curve, not a strict Pareto improvement. (Source: results.md, CWAR Pass@16 not yet computed / trade-off accounting)
- **Whether the C2~S2 mechanism-equivalence story survives on the math-specialized base is undetermined, because S2 there did not shift the operating point away from S0, which could collapse the claim into "base/S0/S2/C2 all statistically equivalent" pending C2 K=32 confirmation.** -- A null operating-point shift would convert the mechanism-equivalence claim into a separate result (asymmetric reward damped to noise on a math-specialized base). (Source: results.md, 'C2-vs-S2 threshold-mechanism story (preview)')

(Source: FINAL_VERDICT.md, Part 2 '5-step plan')
